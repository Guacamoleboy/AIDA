# A visit from Engestofte Gods.

Today we had a company presentation from **Engestofte Gods**. Johan presented how the business works and which problems take up time in their everyday work.

The business is mainly focused on weddings and conferences. They have around five employees and a gardener during the season. Johan lives in Valby, while the estate is in Lolland, so travelling to the location is also something that takes time whenever there is an activity or a problem that needs his attention.

Their work is very dependent on details. A wedding is a large event, and a small mistake can have consequences for the couple, the employees and the company. The average wedding has around 85 guests, and they normally book one or two years in advance. This means that their planning has to be accurate long before the actual event takes place.

### Trello is already important

Johan uses Trello as a Kanban system for almost everything at the estate. A wedding can be seen as a parent issue with smaller child issues underneath it. “Food” could be one area, with tasks for vegetarian guests, allergies and other details. When everything is completed, the wedding is ready to happen.

What stood out to me was that Johan does not want us to replace Trello. He already knows the tool and wants to keep using it. The problem is that the same information often has to be written in several places. When something changes, he has to remember to update every relevant card. If one of the cards is forgotten, the information becomes unreliable.

He wants a **source of truth**. Somewhere he can update the information once, while the relevant Trello cards and communication are kept in sync. That sounds like a much more useful direction than creating yet another project management tool.

### The Christmas market

The Christmas market is currently managed through PDF files, email and Trello. Lise can receive around 1,000 emails during the period, even though many of them contain information that could have been handled through a much smaller process.

One possible idea would be a digital map where vendors can choose and book a stand themselves. A booked position would then be closed automatically. The system would also need rules, because three stands selling æbleskiver next to each other is probably not a great experience for the market.

I found this case interesting because it has an obvious potential for automation, but it also has many practical details. It would need to be simple for the vendors and still give the employees control over the layout and the final approval.

### My first thoughts

The presentation gave me a better understanding of the difference between an interesting AI idea and a useful business solution. Johan does not just need an AI chatbot. He needs less repeated work, fewer opportunities for mistakes and a system that fits the tools he already uses.

I think a solution around Trello could be a good direction. For example, one structured source of truth could contain the event information and then use the Trello API to update cards, checklists or messages. The goal would not be to hide Trello from him, but to stop him from having to enter the same information again and again.

The emails should also feel personal. A wedding is not just another ticket in a system, so an automated response should still contain the relevant details and have a human tone.

The presentation gave me enough inspiration to start thinking about a project. I did not have a final idea yet, but I knew I wanted to solve a real problem instead of building something just because it was technically possible.
