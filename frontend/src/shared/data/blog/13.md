# Legal and ethical considerations in my project

The AIDA lesson on legal and ethical considerations covers responsible AI use, privacy, bias and risk. The course material also points to sustainability, the EU AI Act, RAG and copyright. I wanted to connect those subjects to the Engestofte Gods project instead of treating them as a checklist detached from the product. ([AIDA lesson](https://aida.kursusmaterialer.dk/11-juridiske-etiske-overvejelser/))

### The data is part of the product decision

The enquiry flow asks for a name, wedding date, guest numbers and free-text wishes. Customers may also enter allergies or dietary requirements. The browser stores an unfinished draft in `localStorage`, and each submitted answer is sent to the Java backend and used in an OpenAI request. This means privacy needs to be considered in the actual flow: what we collect, where it goes, who can see it, and when it should be removed.

Some allergy details may reveal information about a person's health. That makes data minimisation and access control especially important. A production solution would need to identify its lawful basis, provide a clear privacy notice, define retention and deletion rules, and assess whether a data protection impact assessment is needed. The prototype is not a legal approval to process real customer data. These decisions need to be reviewed against the GDPR and the actual deployment. ([GDPR, Articles 5, 6, 9, 13 and 35](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng))

The OpenAI API request sets `store` to `false`, but that does not mean the project can skip provider due diligence. OpenAI documents separate default abuse-monitoring retention and other data controls. Before production, the organisation would need to review the exact processing terms, settings, transfers and retention that apply to its account and use. ([OpenAI API data controls](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data))

### Be clear about the AI's role

Customers should know when they are speaking with an AI assistant. The EU AI Act includes transparency obligations for people interacting with AI systems, with the relevant rules applying from 2 August 2026. In this project, that means making the AI role clear and distinguishing an AI-generated response from a confirmed answer from Engestofte. ([EU AI Act, Article 50](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng); [European Commission guidance](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-ai-transparency-obligations))

The assistant can gather information, ask follow-up questions and prepare an internal assessment. It must not promise a booking, invent availability or decide whether Engestofte accepts an enquiry. The Owner remains responsible for confirming business facts and making the final decision. Keeping that boundary visible is both an ethical choice and a useful product rule.

### Errors, bias and human review

Structured JSON makes the response easier for the application to handle, but a valid JSON object is not proof that its contents are true. A model can misunderstand a date, miss a conflict or phrase a dietary need incorrectly. The backend validates the response shape and values, while the Owner reviews the completed enquiry before it becomes an event.

I would test the flow with different languages, ambiguous dates, conflicting answers and unusual dietary requests. The system should ask for clarification or mark uncertainty instead of guessing. This use supports an administrative conversation; it should not be treated as an automated decision about who deserves a wedding date or service. Its exact regulatory classification still depends on the intended use and deployment.

### Copyright, RAG and sustainability

The current MVP does not use RAG. If I later add retrieval from venue documents, menus or package material, I need to make sure Engestofte owns the content or has permission to use it. Making a document searchable does not remove copyright restrictions. ([Directive (EU) 2019/790, Article 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A32019L0790))

The flow also makes an OpenAI call after each submitted answer and sends the conversation context needed to assess it. A responsible next step would be to measure latency and usage, choose a model that is sufficient for the task, and avoid unnecessary repeated context where it can be done without losing important details. I should not claim an energy saving without measuring it.

### My conclusion

Legal and ethical considerations affect the design, not just the final deployment. The prototype demonstrates a useful AI-supported intake flow, but real customer data would require a separate review of privacy, provider processing, security, transparency and retention. AI can make the first conversation easier; it does not take responsibility away from Engestofte.
